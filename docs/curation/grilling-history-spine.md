# Spine — the history of barbecue (`grilling-history-1`)

The canonical narrative, written **source-blind**. Nothing in `data/` was read to
produce this: not the segment pool, not the ASR manifest, not the catalogue, not
a transcript. Only `#226` and `docs/curation/segment-length-rules.md`.

That constraint is the point, and it is worth stating why in the document itself
so nobody later "improves" the spine by checking it against the tape. The
previous `grilling-history-1` drifted into Welsh bakestones, medieval manners and
18th-century dinner parties because its outline was assembled from the tape that
happened to exist. An outliner who can see the catalogue writes an outline the
catalogue fits, and after that no downstream filter can recover the plot, because
the plot has already been bent. So the spine is written first and blind, and the
tape is scored against it.

**Status:** proposed. This is stage 1 of the four stages in #226. Stage 2 sources
each beat independently against that beat's claim; stage 3 reports coverage;
stage 4 assembles in spine order under `segment-length-rules.md`.

**This spine is expected to have holes.** A beat with no tape is a narration
beat, not a beat to be deleted or substituted for. §5 lists the beats I predict
will be hardest to source — as a *prediction for the coverage report*, not a
filter. Nothing was dropped or shortened on a guess about availability, and
guessing at availability is the contamination this document exists to prevent.

## 1. The through-line

"History of barbecue" is a subject. Here is the argument.

> **Barbecue is the trade of time for money: it turns cheap, tough protein into
> abundance using fire, smoke and hours instead of cash. That trade has two
> consequences that repeat everywhere it appears, and the whole history falls out
> of them. Because it takes hours, it cannot be one person's weekday dinner — it
> needs a crowd, so it becomes a social institution. And because those hours are
> hot, skilled and punishing, every barbecue culture has to answer the question
> of *who tends the fire* — which is always, underneath, a question about power.**

Two clauses, and they carry different halves of the document.

**Follow the fire and you get technique.** Combustion, smoke chemistry, collagen,
fuel species, the difference between a skewer and a pit. This is why the
traditions resemble each other: they are independent solutions to the same
physics, and the local variables are fuel, animal and faith.

**Follow the labour and you get history.** Somebody stayed up all night. In the
American case that somebody was, for two centuries, enslaved, and then Black and
poorly paid, and the credit went elsewhere — so the American story is not "and
also there is a difficult chapter about slavery." Enslaved and freed Black
cooks are the **authors** of American barbecue, and the modern era is best
understood as the long re-pricing of their labour: converted into suburban
leisure, then into competitive sport, then into chef prestige, and finally into a
contested heritage claim that the craft revival is still arguing about.

The arc the beats follow:

**fire → preservation → surplus → the world's parallel answers → America's
particular answer and who was made to give it → the re-pricing of that labour**

Two tests this through-line has to keep passing, because they are what the old
outline failed:

- **The relevance test is the beat, not the topic.** A segment is not admitted
  for being about food, history or cooking. It is admitted for advancing the
  claim of the beat it is assigned to (#226, founder rule 4).
- **Length is an output.** The proportions in §3 are pacing guidance for a
  ~2–3 hour Foray. They are not quotas, and a beat that comes in at half its
  share because that is all the good tape there was has not failed.

## 2. Where the story chains, and where it fans

This matters to a listener more than it looks like it should, because a jump
inside a chain and a jump inside a fan feel completely different. A chain break
reads as a gap — the listener notices something missing. A fan break reads as a
change of subject, which is fine, but a fan that is *not signalled* as a fan
reads as the narrative losing its place. That is close to what the founder heard
in the bakestone version.

| Act | beats | structure | what the listener should feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| I — Fire, smoke, mechanism | 1–5 | **chain**, causal | each beat is caused by the one before |
| II — Surplus and power | 6–7 | **chain**, causal | the social consequence of Act I |
| III — The world's parallel answers | 8–15 | **fan**, parallel | deliberate stops on a tour; no beat depends on its neighbour |
| IV — The American case | 16–29 | **chain**, chronological | a single line, 1492 to about 1970 |
| V — The re-pricing | 30–38 | **chain**, chronological | 1945 to now |

Three notes on the seams, all of which are assembly instructions as much as
editorial ones:

- **Act III is a fan and must be announced as one.** Narration entering beat 8
  should say plainly that the next stretch is a tour of traditions that do not
  descend from each other, and that the American thread resumes afterwards. Then
  a fan beat with no tape costs the listener nothing — a missing stop on a tour
  is invisible, where a missing link in a chain is a hole. **This means Act III
  is the safest place in the spine for the coverage report to come back thin, and
  Act IV is the most dangerous.**
- **The fan is ordered by lineage, not geography.** Beats 8–12 are the
  direct-heat skewer family, roughly oldest surviving technology first; beats
  13–15 are the whole-animal communal-fire family. Both orderings are defensible;
  what is not defensible is a random walk around the map, because a listener will
  try to infer a principle from the order and will be misled by an arbitrary one.
- **The fan's exit is engineered.** Beat 15 (jerk) is the last fan stop because
  it belongs to both structures: it is a parallel Caribbean tradition *and* the
  living descendant of the technique the English word came from. It hands
  straight to beat 16. If the fan is reordered, keep jerk last.

Act IV's chain has one internal division worth flagging: beats 25–27 (Texas, the
Carolinas, Kansas City and Memphis) are themselves a small fan inside the chain —
parallel regional divergences, not a sequence — and beat 28 is the beat that
turns them back into an argument. Do not cut beat 28; without it the three region
beats are a listicle.

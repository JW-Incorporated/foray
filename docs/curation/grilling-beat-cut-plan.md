# Beat cut plan — making the barbecue Foray fundable

The assembly consequence of `narration-craft.md`. That document establishes that **the
complete 40-beat barbecue Foray, written honestly on today's coverage, is about 82 minutes
long and 44 % narrator** — past its own R-essay line — and that the enforcement rule is
**cut beats, not words**, with fan stops going first and chain links never.

This document works that rule against the real spine and the real coverage report and says
which beats an assembly should not attempt. Its companion is
`grilling-history-coverage.md` §10, which classifies every empty and thin beat by depth
mode, writability and budget. **§10 says what each beat would cost. This document says
which ones we can afford.**

**Status:** proposed. No narration was written, no spine was edited, no API was called, and
nothing in `data/` was touched.

---

## 0. The finding, stated first because it is a refusal

> **The barbecue Foray cannot be made fundable at 40 beats without cutting beats the spine
> calls load-bearing. It cannot be made fundable at 40 beats at all.**

The full arithmetic is in §2 and §3. The short form:

- **Cutting every legally cuttable beat in the spine — all nine of them — leaves the Foray
  at 35.2 %**, which is over R-foray's hard ceiling of 35 %. Those nine cuts cost **zero
  seconds of tape**, so there is no cheaper move available and no better one.
- **Reaching the 35 % ceiling then requires cutting one chain link. Reaching the 25 %
  target requires cutting eight.** §4c of `narration-craft.md` forbids both: "chain links
  are never dropped."
- So the rule cannot be satisfied on today's tape at 40 beats. **What can be satisfied is
  the rule's own premise**, and §4b states it: "the number of beats a Foray can carry is a
  function of how much tape it has, not of how many holes its spine has. Tape funds
  narration." There are therefore exactly two honest routes — buy tape, or ship a smaller
  Foray on purpose — and §4 recommends the second now and the first next.
- The recommended ship-now assembly is **22 beats, 53.6 minutes, 24.8 % narrator**, which
  is inside the target with room. It cuts nine fan-class beats legally and **nine chain
  links in breach of §4c**, and §4 states that breach rather than hiding it.

**One thing this document will not do, and it is the reason it says all of the above.**
There is a way to keep all forty beats under the 35 % ceiling: write every Carry at the
45-second floor instead of the 80-second planning figure. That lands at **34.95 %**, under
the ceiling by four hundredths of a point. It is arithmetically real and it is exactly the
move §4c exists to forbid — "over-ceiling is fixed by cutting beats, not words" — and the
alcohol spine names the same error in the same paragraph: "above all do not compensate by
lengthening a neighbour." Twenty beats each written a third shorter than the argument needs
is forty beats of rushed steps. **It is recorded here so that nobody proposes it as a
discovery.**

### 1a. A correction to the base, and it makes the problem slightly worse

§4b's fourth caveat notices that this base is not assemblable, because the six Grill Coach
segments are 684.4 s of 2,737.4 s — **25.002 %** — fractionally over M4, the 25 %
single-episode concentration cap in `segment-length-rules.md` §6c. That is right.

**It misses a second breach, and the second one is not fractional.** M4 caps an episode at
25 % of a Foray's segments *or* 25 % of its runtime, and the base holds **seven Stuff You
Should Know cuts of twenty-five segments — 28.0 %.** SYSK is comfortable on runtime at
20.9 % and over the cap on count. So the base breaches M4 twice, on two different episodes,
by two different measures, and any assembly built from it has to shed tape before it starts.
§3 does that shedding explicitly.

**Note the direction, because it is the same direction as every other caveat in §4b.** Less
playable tape means a smaller Carry budget and a higher narration share. **The 44 % figure
is a floor.**

### 1b. One lever that is already spent, so that nobody spends it twice

The coverage report's §5b holds out a genuine free win: four Grill Coach segments — beats
18, 24 and both beat 38 cuts — are authored, anchor-verified and held back from
`grilling-history-2` only because M4 will not admit them at that Foray's length, and "any
future re-assembly that lengthens this Foray... gains beats 18, 24 and 38 for no further
sourcing work at all."

That is true of `grilling-history-2`. **It is not an improvement available to the plan
below, because the base above already counts all six Grill Coach segments.** The 44 %
finding is computed on a tape pool that has already banked the free win. Worth stating
plainly, because "we still have four segments in hand" is the first thing a reader of §5b
will reach for.

## 3. The impossibility, proved

After stage one the droppable inventory is empty. Every fan stop in the spine is cut, the
internal fan is cut, and the cross-cutting beat is cut. **What remains empty is twelve
beats, and all twelve are chain links or the coda.**

The Carry budget at each bound, on the stage-one machinery of 524 s and the tape base of
2,737.4 s. `narration_allowed = tape × ceiling / (1 − ceiling)`, which for a share bound of
25 % is simply `tape / 3`:

| bound | narration allowed | less machinery | **empty beats carryable** | of 12 needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| target 25 % | 912.5 s | 388.5 s | **4** | 8 short |
| ceiling 35 % | 1,474.3 s | 950.3 s | **11** | **1 short** |

Checked at the boundaries rather than trusted: at 4 Carries the share is 23.57 % and at 5 it
is 25.24 %, so 4 is the target's true limit; at 11 it is 33.90 % and at 12 it is 35.15 %, so
11 is the ceiling's.

**The ceiling is missed by exactly one carried chain link.** That is the sharpest way to say
what is wrong with this Foray, and it is worth pausing on: after nine free cuts, the honest
40-beat spine is *one beat* over the line past which it stops being a work made of
authorities. The target is missed by eight.

### 3a. What would close the gap, and the exchange rate is not what it looks like

Two ways to fund the twelve, and they are not equally efficient. Both assume a new segment
is 180 s, the top of the tape target band, which is the generous reading.

**Buy tape that lands elsewhere**, leaving all twelve beats empty and merely funding them.
Each new segment adds 180 s of tape and 7 s of seam:

```
narration = 1,484 + 7n   ≤   (2,737.4 + 180n) / 3   →   n ≥ 10.8
```

**Eleven new segments — about 33 minutes of tape, across at least four distinct episodes for
M4 — and the result is a 105-minute Foray at 24.9 %.**

**Or buy tape that closes the holes**, which is the same money spent better. Each beat closed
gains 180 s of tape *and* sheds 80 s of Carry:

```
narration = 1,484 − 73m   ≤   (2,737.4 + 180m) / 3   →   m ≥ 4.3
```

**Five beats closed — five segments, about 15 minutes of tape — and the result is a
79-minute Foray at 23.5 %,** with all twelve chain links intact and nothing cut but the nine
fan-class beats.

| | segments to buy | resulting runtime | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| fund the holes from elsewhere | **11** | 104.6 min | 24.9 % |
| **close five of the holes** | **5** | 79.3 min | 23.5 % |

**One hole closed is worth about two segments bought anywhere else**, and that is the number
this project should be planning against. §4b's version of this — "sourcing one more strong
episode buys about 180 s of tape, which buys about 97 s of narration at the ceiling, which
buys one more carried beat" — is the weaker half of the truth. Tape aimed at an empty beat
does two jobs; tape aimed anywhere else does one.

## 4. The recommendation — a 22-beat Foray at 24.8 %

Stage one is not shippable and buying tape is not a thing this document can do. So the
recommendation is the spine's own remedy: **fewer beats, deliberately.** §4c of the spine
already authorises it — "the short mode is fewer beats, never shortened segments" — and the
alcohol spine says the quiet part: "a shorter Foray is a legitimate outcome."

The construction principle is the one §4b implies and nobody has yet applied: **include every
beat that brings tape, because tape funds narration, and then spend what is left on chain
links.** A beat with tape is not a cost centre. It is the thing paying for the Carries.

### 4c. It satisfies the rules the 40-beat version breaks, and that is the argument for it

- **No two adjacent empty beats anywhere.** Beat 3 sits between strong beats 1 and 4; beat 16
  between strong 15 and 18; beat 20 between thin 19 and strong 21; beat 31 between thin 30 and
  strong 32; beat 40 after strong 38. So §2e's merge rule never fires and the two-item
  consecutive cap is never approached — where the 40-beat spine breaks the cap outright at
  10–13 and again at 33–36.
- **Two placement constraints, recorded because they are load-bearing.** Beat 7's Patch must
  precede its tape, since the beat exists so the power clause arrives before Act IV; so beat
  6's Patch must also precede its own tape, or 6-then-7 is two narration items in a row with
  the second longer than the first. Same for beat 30: its Patch goes *before* the Atlanta
  anecdote, which is the right order anyway — the pattern, then the instance.
- **Beats 21 and 22 survive with their strong tape, consecutively, in the same voice, in tape
  order**, which is what §5b of the coverage report chose them for.
- **D1** at 53.6 min allows 5 starts per rolling 600 s; 21 segments average 3.9. The worst
  window still needs checking at assembly time.

### 4d. What it costs, said plainly

**Eighteen beats are not attempted.** Nine are fan-class and legal:

> 8, 10, 11, 12, 13 (Act III), 26, 27, 28 (the internal fan), 33 (cross-cutting)

**Nine are chain links, and cutting them breaks the rule this document cites:**

> 2, 17, 23, 25, 29, 34, 35, 36, 39

One of those nine — beat 23 — is forced by the gate rather than the budget, because its Patch
is unwritable and its tape cannot ship without it. **The other eight are cut for money.** That
is precisely the count §3 predicted, which is the one consolation available: the plan does not
break the rule any harder than the arithmetic requires.

What the listener loses, in the spine's own terms. **Act III keeps three stops of eight** — the
skewer family, the asado and jerk — so the entire radiant and table-top family goes, along
with the fan's control case and its one non-American case of fire bound to national identity.
The spine warned about that last one directly: cut beat 13 and "the American reckoning looks
parochial." **Act IV loses its prologue's second half and its entire regional stretch**, so
Texas, the Carolinas, Kansas City and Memphis — the four styles the founder named — are absent,
and beat 29's argument about why the map has the shape it does goes with them. **Act V loses
four of its ten beats**, including the gas appliance that closes beat 4's loop and the
competition circuit that explains how modern barbecue tastes.

**What survives is the through-line.** Fire and mechanism, the crowd and the power, three world
answers, the word, the inheritance, the labour system, the archive, the enterprise, the policy,
the manufactured backyard, the inversion, the unconcluded argument, and the coda. **The two
clauses of §1 of the spine both arrive, and the authorship argument arrives on tape rather than
on our own credit.** That is the Foray worth shipping on today's coverage, and it is a
different and smaller thing than the spine describes.

### 4e. Against the spine's own 18-beat short version

§4c of the spine already proposes a short spine: 1, 2, 3, 6, 13 or 14, 15, 16, 20, 21, 22, 24,
27, 29, 31, 32, 37, 38, 40. It was written source-blind, so it is worth checking against what
the tape turned out to be, and the comparison is instructive rather than critical.

**It does not clear the target, and it does not clear M4 either.** Five of its eighteen beats
would be carried by Grill Coach segments — 21, 22, 24 and two on 38 — which is **31 % of its
segments and 28 % of its runtime**, a double M4 breach larger than the base's. Satisfying M4
costs it two of those five, so beat 24 falls back to SYSK and beat 38 loses its contest half,
leaving **1,650 s of tape across 14 segments and 802 s of narration: 40.9 minutes at
32.71 %** — under the ceiling, over the target. Two of its choices are now known to be wrong
on the evidence: **beat 27 is unwritable and has no tape of its own**, and **beat 2's only
candidate is committed to beat 15**, so two of its eighteen beats are Carries where the short
spine assumed content.

**The 22-beat plan is the same instinct, sourced.** It keeps every beat of the short version
that has tape or is cheaply writable, drops the two that turned out hollow, and spends the
difference on beats 4, 5, 7, 9, 18, 19, 30 — all of which have tape the short version leaves on
the floor. That is the whole lesson: **a short spine chosen source-blind and a short spine
chosen after sourcing are different documents, and the second is the assembly's.**

## 5. What changes the answer, in order of how much it changes it

1. **The SYSK register ruling** (coverage §2b, §9 item 9). The plan's margin is 0.19 points and
   five of its twenty-one segments are SYSK. Rule the register out and 491.3 s of tape leaves,
   beats 7, 19 and 30 return to empty and become Carries rather than Patches, and the plan
   lands near **31 %** — under the ceiling, over the target — and needs re-assembling rather
   than recomputing, because Grill Coach would then hold 5 of 16 segments and breach M4 again.
   **This is not a sensitivity, it is a dependency.** It also removes the prior tape beat 29's
   synthesis attribution would have relied on, per coverage §10e.
2. **Five sourced segments aimed at empty chain links** (§3a). Buys back all nine chain links
   this plan cuts and lands a 31-beat Foray at 23.5 %. **This is the recommendation that
   matters**, and it is a sourcing action rather than an editorial one.
3. **A founder ruling on R-foray itself.** `narration-craft.md` §9 already flags that 25 / 35 /
   40 are judgement rather than arithmetic. At a 30 % target the stage-one 31-beat Foray funds
   **8 Carries against the 12 it needs** — so it still cuts four chain links. At 35 % it cuts
   one. **No plausible loosening of the target rescues 40 beats**, which is worth knowing before
   the number is renegotiated.
4. **Minting the beat 4 cut** (coverage §9 item 7). Already assumed present in every figure
   here, per figure C. If it is not minted, beat 4 is thin rather than strong and the plan loses
   108.9 s of tape.

## 6. Recommendations that are not this document's to make

### 6a. Spine amendments — recommended, not made

Two are already pending in the coverage report's §8, on beats 22 and 27. A third is
recommended here, and one of the two pending ones gains a second reason.

1. **Beat 33 — narrow it or accept it is out.** Coverage §10f finds it the one unwritable empty
   beat in the spine: two of its four comparative legs are themselves empty beats, and its
   payload claim about women in Black barbecue businesses is sourced nowhere in the corpus. A
   narrower beat confined to the two legs the Foray plays — the asador as a social office
   against the postwar grill's advertising — would be writable and would be a smaller beat.
   §6d's third route applies: "a beat whose claim cannot be written *or* sourced may be a beat
   whose claim is wrong."
2. **Beat 27 — a second reason to touch it.** §8's pending amendment is about the German mustard
   trace, which the tape contradicts. Coverage §10e adds that the beat's *reason to belong* —
   the contested-custody half — is unwritable from this corpus, and that beat 38's tape, which is
   the right material, plays later than R3's synthesis exception can reach back.
3. **Beat 23 — the seating claim needs a source or the beat needs restating.** Not a
   spine error, and worth distinguishing from the two above: the claim looks true and is simply
   unsourced here. But as written the beat cannot ship, because it may not ship without the
   seating and the seating cannot be attributed. §8's second finding — that the spine has no
   beat for the 19th-century westward diffusion — is the place to argue this, since a beat for
   the diffusion would carry `#2572` squarely where beat 23 carries it thinly.

**No amendment is proposed to beats 21, 22 or 40**, and none to the act structure.

### 6b. Asks on `narration-craft.md`

1. **§2d needs four Carry provenances, not two.** By-default and by-design do not cover four of
   this Foray's twenty empty beats: 11, 12 and 17 are empty **by ruling**, and 13 is by-default
   with the avenue **exhausted** on a clean negative. The distinction is operational, because
   §2d's rejection posture turns on whether sourcing can help. Coverage §10a carries the table.
2. **§4d's per-beat model does not see two real costs.** A thin beat whose only candidate is
   committed to another beat has no tape, so it costs a Carry and not a Patch — that is beats 2,
   6 and 27, and 780 characters becoming 1,360 three times over. And a Carry told to absorb its
   neighbours' material is at the soft max, not the mid-band — that is beat 29 at about 2,550
   characters.
3. **§4b's M4 caveat is half a caveat.** It catches the Grill Coach runtime breach at 25.002 %
   and misses the larger SYSK one at 7 of 25 segments, 28.0 %. See §1a.
4. **§2e's merge rule does not say whether it reaches a fan.** Act III's beats 10 to 13 are four
   consecutive empty beats in a fan, which breaks the two-item consecutive cap on any reading.
   Merging them would breach the 180 s hard max at 320 s and would put four unrelated traditions
   in one item, in a structure whose stops are supposed to be announced separately.
5. **A question for whoever owns M4's denominator, and it is not rhetorical.** Does a Foray's
   *runtime* include its narration items? Every figure in this document assumes not, because that
   is how the coverage report's §5b computed it and how `grilling-history-2` behaves today. If
   narration does count, **the concentration cap loosens as narration grows** — a Foray can admit
   more of one episode by narrating more — which is a perverse incentive worth ruling out
   explicitly rather than discovering.

### 6c. Where the cut/spine distinction has to live in the data

**Cutting a beat is not deleting it from the spine.** The spine is the canonical narrative,
written source-blind, and it is not a record of what any assembly managed to play. A cut beat is
one *this assembly does not attempt*, and the same beat may be attempted by the next one — beats
18, 24 and 38 are already the precedent, held back from one Foray and available to another.

So the field belongs on the **assembly**, scoped to the Foray, and nowhere else:

- **On the Foray record**, an `omitted_beats[]` of `{ beat_id, reason }`, with `reason` drawn
  from a closed set: `fan_stop_cut_for_ratio`, `chain_link_cut_for_ratio`, `unwritable`,
  `tape_withdrawn`. The distinction between the first two is the whole point — one is
  enforcement working and the other is enforcement failing — and a single `cut` value would
  destroy exactly the information this document exists to report. **Recommended as a schema ask,
  because `data/forays.json` is not this document's to edit.**
- **On the coverage report**, `writable` / `unwritable` as the second axis on empty beats (§6d's
  own ask, now carried in coverage §10) plus `carry_provenance` from §6b item 1. These are
  properties of the beat against the corpus, not of an assembly, so they belong here and not on
  the Foray.
- **Never on the spine.** Recording an assembly outcome in the spine is the contamination the
  spine's own opening paragraph forbids: "an outliner who can see the catalogue writes an outline
  the catalogue fits." A spine annotated with what shipped is a spine that has seen the tape.
- **Not conflated with a rejected script.** `narration-craft.md` §6c requires a
  `rejection_reason` on a rejected script that is never deleted. **An omitted beat and a rejected
  script are different events** — one is "we did not try", the other is "we tried and it failed
  the gate" — and a pipeline that stores them in one field cannot tell a fan stop dropped for
  budget from a Carry that came back as filler.

**And the reason must never be deleted**, per §6c's discipline of label, never exclude. The next
assembly needs to know that beat 13 was cut because Act III could not be funded and not because
braai does not matter.

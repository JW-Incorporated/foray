# Persona-rating pilot (RV-13), 2026-10-14

Card RV-13 of [`docs/roadmap/recommender-v1.md`](../roadmap/recommender-v1.md), issue #1380. The orchestrator ran it on the subscription, as plan decision D1 specifies: Claude Code agents (Sonnet 5.5) followed [`docs/agents/runner-prompts/persona-rate-batch.md`](../agents/runner-prompts/persona-rate-batch.md) v1, with no API call and no network access during rating. The block between the `agreement:pilot` markers is generated by `tools/recommender/agreement.mjs`; everything outside it is written by hand.

## Verdict: GO for RV-19, with three changes

1. **Keep the joint design: one call rates all 13 personas.** Every persona fails the plan's fixed bar, which is joint-vs-isolated Spearman >= 0.9. But that bar cannot be met even by the joint design against itself: two joint passes over the same 59 shows reach only 0.74-0.96 per persona (median 0.86), because about half of all fits are 1 and ties depress rank correlation (table below). Measured against each persona's own retest ceiling, most personas are within noise. Mean fit moves by 0.25 or less between joint and isolated, and 68-93% of fits match exactly. Isolated rating costs **ten times as much per show-rating** (about 7.5k tokens against 0.7k), so a fully isolated gold run is not justified.
2. **Re-measure the personas that fall below their own ceiling, at a usable n.** The clear cases are slow-news-contextualist (0.667 against a retest of 0.827) and true-crime-careful (0.649 against 0.859). Two more sit just past the line: banter-regular (0.807 against 0.961) and practical-improver (0.740 against 0.861). RV-19 keeps an isolated slice of 100 gold shows for these four, about 20 batches and 3M tokens. **New rule** (replacing the fixed 0.9): a persona is flagged when its joint-vs-isolated Spearman falls more than 0.10 below its own joint-vs-joint retest Spearman. If a persona is still flagged at n = 100, a follow-up card adds a persona-group mode to `prepare-rating-batch.mjs`, and only that persona is re-rated, in its own group call.
3. **Lock the progress file before RV-19.** `prepare-rating-batch.mjs` and `merge-rating-results.mjs` each rewrite `data/recommender/rating-progress.json` whole. Running a prepare while a pool merged lost four batches' `markRated` records. The ratings themselves were safe, and re-running the idempotent merge restored the records. The pilot serialised merges with a lock-directory wrapper, but prepare did not take the lock. Follow-up: one lock inside `progress.mjs` that both scripts use.

## Reading the numbers

- **Consistency.** Test-retest alpha on `fit` is 0.908 overall and 0.83-0.97 per persona, above the 0.80 gate. The raters are consistent, which the research predicted, so the question is bias, not noise.
- **Distribution (the bias to watch).** Fit is heavily skewed: about 52% of all persona fits are 1, and the overall mean is 1.74. Six personas are flagged as compressed (more than 60% at one value). The largest are true-crime-careful at 79% ones and sharp-comedy at 75%. **This mostly reflects the catalogue, not the rubric:** a stratified random sample of 26k shows contains few true-crime or comedy shows, and a narrow persona rejects almost everything outside its topic. Fives are rare (49 of 3,887). For the student model (RV-14), the target is dominated by 1s, so its gate should be read alongside a per-persona check on the 3+ subset. The listener-facing value of a rating is in its upper tail.
- **Differentiation is strong.** The variance ratio is 9.3: personas disagree about a show far more than shows differ on average. No pair correlates at r >= 0.85. The closest are narrative-binger/true-crime-careful (0.68) and banter-regular/sharp-comedy (0.62), both intended adjacencies. Three personas are rarely the top persona: family-listener 2.3%, sharp-comedy 1.0%, true-crime-careful 1.3%. That is the same catalogue base-rate effect, not redundancy. **Recommendation for RV-29: keep all 13 personas for RV-19.** Nothing duplicates another; low top-share personas serve real listeners, who are rare in a random catalogue sample, not in the app.
- **Top persona changes** between passes for 32% of retest shows. Almost all of these are near-ties among low fits (2 against 2 or 3). `top_reason` should only be shown when the top persona's fit is 4 or more.
- **Rubric hygiene.**
  - Confidence caps and audio nulls were never triggered (`confidence_capped` 0, `audio_nulled` 0): raters respect the evidence tiers unprompted.
  - Injection: 0 flags over 300 shows, and the pre-scan flagged none. One rater noted leaked AI boilerplate in an episode description that did not address a rater, which is correctly not flagged.
  - Content flags: explicit 14, medical/financial advice 8, harassment 0.
  - Formats: interview 102, mixed 62, panel 51, narrative 42, monologue 28, news 14.
- **Commute framing slips through as "trip".** The ban is literal (commute, drive), so raters wrote "far shorter than a full trip" and "long for a family trip". 17 reasons use "trip" or "journey" (some legitimately, as in "personal journey"). Follow-up for the rubric and `schema.mjs`: ban "trip" in the time-fit sense, or require length reasons to be stated in minutes alone.
- **Pack defects found by raters.** The Race F1 Podcast's blurb describes Hollywood gossip. Show 1790108248 is tagged automotive, but its episodes are Broadway interviews. One show has a mismatched parenting tag. Raters rated these on the episodes, as the rubric asks. These are catalogue-label bugs, and the next RV-26 anomaly audit should list them.

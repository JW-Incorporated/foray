## 0. The two findings that reshape the plan

### 0.1 Transcripts and stable timelines never co-occur

Measured 2026-08-11 against 208 live curated feeds, **81,553 episodes**,
episode-level (not the show-level figure quoted earlier):

| pool | episodes with a `<podcast:transcript>` |
|---|---|
| **DAI** (ad-stitched) | 7,846 / 58,472 — **13.4%** |
| **non-DAI** (stable timeline) | 166 / 23,081 — **0.7%** |

A 19× gap, pointing the wrong way. #63 §2's v1 plan — *"source segments only
from the non-DAI items"* — targets a pool with effectively no free transcripts.
166 episodes total, most of them a single show (Geology Bites, 120).

The shows with rich free transcripts are all DAI: Stuff You Should Know
2,850/2,858, Odd Lots 1,251/1,255, Las Culturistas 558/558, This Podcast Will
Kill You 297/298.

The correlation is not a coincidence. Transcripts are published by
professionalised operations investing in accessibility and SEO — the same
operations monetising through dynamic ad insertion. Indie static-hosted shows
do neither.

**Consequence:** free boundaries (transcripts) and stable timelines (non-DAI)
are close to mutually exclusive. Under the Max-only constraint, transcripts
must be free, so **the workable pool is DAI shows** — which makes
`ADR-0007` dual anchoring load-bearing rather than a nice-to-have, and puts
**#29 (downloads) on the critical path for Foray playback**.

It also means **extraction and playback decouple**: cataloguing can run for days
and produce durable, correct output before a single Foray is playable. That is
a feature of this sequencing, not a defect — but it must be understood, or the
cataloguing will look like it produced nothing.

### 0.2 Format matters more than availability

Of 15,319 timestamped transcript tags vs 7,900 without:

| format | tags | usable for segments |
|---|---|---|
| `text/vtt` | 7,063 | ✅ |
| `application/srt` | 6,848 | ✅ |
| `text/plain` | 6,632 | ❌ no timestamps |
| `text/html` | 1,268 | ❌ no timestamps |
| `application/json` | 787 | ✅ |
| `application/x-subrip` | 621 | ✅ |

66% usable. Shows typically publish 3–4 formats at once (~2.9 tags per
transcribed episode), so the choice is real, and
`backend/src/feeds/parser.ts:276` currently picks **`text/plain` first** —
the one format that cannot anchor a boundary. That preference was written for
enrichment ("what is this episode about"), where plain text is correct. It is
wrong for segmentation. See **WP-A1**.

## 1. What the Max-only constraint blocks

| Blocked | Why | Free alternative | WP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Whisper transcription** | ~$0.006/audio-min; ~$0.27/episode; ~$20k full corpus. Dollar-bound, not token-bound — Max does not help. | None. The catalogue is limited to episodes that publish a transcript. | — |
| **ElevenLabs narration** | New paid API; unapproved (#64 §2). | Device TTS (`SpeechSynthesis` / `AVSpeechSynthesizer`) or on-screen text bridges. See **WP-A9**. | A9 |

**Narration is the sharper of the two.** A Foray is *segments joined by
narration* — remove the narration and it is a playlist with hard cuts. So the
Max-only path needs an answer, not a deferral. Device TTS is free, requires no
network, hosts nothing (which suits the "legally boring" posture), and the
player's reducer already models `tts` queue items. Its complication is that
synthesis produces no file with a known duration up front, which the queue
model currently assumes. WP-A9 is the ADR that decides this.

Everything else in this scope runs on Max.

### 2.1 The connectors are the biggest trap in the mockup

The onboarding screen promises "Connect an account and we'll learn your
interests automatically" across four providers. Honest feasibility:

| Connector | Reality |
|---|---|
| **Apple Podcasts** — "Import listening history" | **Infeasible.** Apple publishes no public API for a user's podcast library, subscriptions, or listening history, and there is no OAuth surface for it. Nothing to integrate. |
| **Google** — "Interests from your account" | **Infeasible.** No such API exists. |
| **Spotify** — "Import listening history" | **Partial.** `/me/shows` (saved podcast shows) works with `user-library-read`. Listening *history* via `/me/player/recently-played` is tracks-oriented and unreliable for podcasts. So: saved shows yes, history no. |
| **YouTube** — "Import subscriptions" | **Feasible** via Data API `subscriptions.list` + OAuth — but YouTube subscriptions are mostly not podcasts, so signal quality is questionable. |

Two of four cannot be built at all, and the most prominent one (Apple) is the
one a podcast listener would expect most. Shipping that screen as drawn
promises something undeliverable.

**The realistic substitute is OPML import** — the universal podcast
subscription format, exported by Overcast, Pocket Casts, AntennaPod, Castro and
most others. It is already on the roadmap (`06_ROADMAP.md` M7). It is not as
magical as a one-tap connector, but it is real, it is free, it needs no OAuth
with anyone, and it captures exactly the signal the connectors were reaching
for. WP-C5 is the spike that confirms this and redesigns the screen around it.

### 2.2 "Build a Foray in 5 seconds" is not the shape of the work

The Create screen shows a live build: *"Searching 2.4M episodes → Choosing the
strongest segments → Clipping the relevant parts → Recording narrator bridges →
Stitching your Foray."* Roughly 5 seconds of progress steps.

Against the real pipeline, per user request that would mean: fetching and
normalising transcripts, running segment extraction across candidate episodes
(~15–20k tokens *per episode*), and synthesising narration — tens of seconds to
minutes, at meaningful token cost, on demand, per user.

That is the opposite of the architecture: extraction is a **batch, offline,
human-reviewed** process producing a durable segment pool, and assembly is a
cheap selection over that pool. `docs/curation/personalization-and-depth-plan.md`
already made this call for playlists — *"LLM only translates a vague prompt into
a filter spec, never selects content."* The same rule applies here.

**So Create-a-Foray v1 assembles from the existing segment pool**, and is
honest when a subject is not covered yet — the same "not much on X yet" path
`buildPlaylist()` already has. The progress animation can stay; it just narrates
selection, not extraction.

## 4. Distribution — why the split is where it is

**Joey gets whole lanes, not tasks inside Claude's lanes.** Track B is entirely
his and touches only `data/breadth-classification.json`. Track A's tail
(A10–A12) is his and touches only `data/segments.json` plus topic selection.
Neither collides with the tooling Claude is building, because Claude's Track A
work writes *code* under `tools/segments/` and Joey's writes *data* under
`data/`. Different files, different PRs, no rebase pain.

**The gates are genuinely his and Wyatt's, not mine.** C3, C6, C10 and A9 each
push against something already decided — the exploration floor, ADR-0005's
anonymous-first identity, the legal memo's single-user posture, and the
no-unapproved-spend rule. I have written what I think in each issue, but a
mockup is not an amendment to a decision record.

**Sequencing overnight:** A1 → A2 → A3 → A4 are a clean dependency chain with
no gates in front of them, and they are the whole foundation for both Joey's
cataloguing and the client work. That is what gets built first.
